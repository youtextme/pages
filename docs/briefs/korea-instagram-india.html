Why a ranked top-10 failed

The brief asked for top Instagram accounts in South Korea whose public posts show India travel. That requires (a) a complete or sample-biased crawl of Korean Instagram by follower count, (b) geotag or visual verification per post, and (c) stable public access to those posts.

Kill criterion triggered

Without authenticated Instagram search, follower rankings are [unverified]. Web search surfaces news-covered celebrity trips, not a statistically sound “top N.” Publishing a fabricated top-10 would violate this project’s kill criteria.

Instagram location search and follower-sorted people search require logged-in sessions and return incomplete results to automated tools. Third-party “top influencer” lists often omit verification links.

Research method

  • Query: Korean celebrity + India + Instagram (English and Korean keywords).
  • Require ≥2 independent news outlets citing Instagram posts, or 1 tier-1 outlet + official show documentation.
  • Reject influencer marketing lists without primary post links.
  • Attempt Instagram location explore for “India” — blocked without login; logged result as inconclusive.
  • As-of stamp: 2026-08-30 UTC.

What would count as proof next time

  • Post-level URL (instagram.com/p/…) archived on Wayback Machine showing geotag or caption naming an Indian locality.
  • Location tag on the post matching a Google Maps place in India.
  • Follower rank from Instagram’s category search with screenshot + date, or licensed social-data provider with methodology disclosed.
  • Minimum N — e.g., top 10 by followers among accounts with ≥1 archived India post in the last 24 months.

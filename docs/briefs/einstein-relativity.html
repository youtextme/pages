Einstein’s relativity: zero to hero (Galileo → GPS)

Relativity is the demand that the laws of physics look the same in every inertial frame while light’s speed in vacuum stays fixed — from that single tension, time dilation, E = mc², curved spacetime, and GPS corrections all follow.

Inertial frames and Galilean intuition

An inertial frame is a reference frame with no net acceleration — a train at constant velocity counts; a spinning carousel does not. Galileo noted that below decks you cannot tell steady motion from rest: physics experiments give the same results (Stanford SEP on simultaneity conventions discusses how Einstein sharpened this).

Classically, velocities add: if you walk 5 km/h inside a train doing 100 km/h, ground observers see 105 km/h. Time and length were assumed absolute — every clock agrees on “now.”

Where classical physics breaks

Maxwell’s equations predict a fixed speed c ≈ 299,792 km/s for light in vacuum. If you chase a light beam, classical velocity addition says you should see a slower beam — experiments (and Einstein’s 1905 postulate) say you do not.

Special relativity: c, time dilation, E = mc²

Einstein’s 1905 paper *Zur Elektrodynamik bewegter Körper* starts from two postulates: (1) physics laws are the same in all inertial frames; (2) c is the same in all inertial frames, independent of the source’s motion.

Symbols you will see: c = speed of light in vacuum; v = relative speed between frames; t = time in the “rest” frame; t′ = time in the moving frame. The Lorentz factor γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²) converts between frames.

Time dilation: A clock moving at speed v relative to you ticks slower by factor γ. Proper time Δτ on the moving clock relates to your elapsed time Δt via Δt = γ Δτ. At everyday speeds γ ≈ 1; at GPS satellite speeds the effect is small but measurable.

E = mc²: Einstein’s follow-on 1905 note showed mass and energy are equivalent — E is energy, m is rest mass, c links the units. A small mass defect converts to enormous energy because c² is huge. This is not “everything is light”; it is a statement about how much energy is stored in mass at rest.

Simultaneity: Einstein showed there is no absolute “now” for separated events — synchronizing distant clocks requires a convention (e.g., light-signal midpoint rule). That is why special relativity pairs space and time into spacetime.

General relativity: equivalence and curvature

By 1907 Einstein had the equivalence principle: in a small enough region, gravitational acceleration is indistinguishable from acceleration of your reference frame (elevator thought experiment). Gravity is not a force in the Newtonian sense acting at a distance — it is geometry.

In November 1915 Einstein presented the final field equations to the Prussian Academy. The decisive communication *Die Feldgleichungen der Gravitation* (25 Nov 1915) completed the theory. Mass-energy tells spacetime how to curve; curved spacetime tells matter how to move.

Curvature in plain language: Parallel lines (geodesics) can converge — that is what we feel as gravity. The 1915 papers recovered the anomalous Mercury perihelion precession, which Newtonian gravity could not fully explain.

For deeper reading without hype: Stanford Encyclopedia — Simultaneity and Conventionality, Einstein Papers Project / MPIWG scans, and undergraduate texts cited in Springer’s historical volume on the November 1915 papers.

What GPS actually does with relativity

GPS satellites carry atomic clocks. Engineers must correct those clocks or range measurements drift kilometers per day. NIST Technical Note 1385 walks through the corrections tied to the constancy of c and coordinate time definitions.

EffectPhysical causeApprox. daily clock shift (satellite vs ground)Relativity type
Gravitational time dilationWeaker gravity higher up ⇒ clocks run faster~+45 µs/dayGeneral (NIST TN 1385)
Kinematic time dilationSatellite speed ⇒ moving clocks run slower~−7 µs/daySpecial (QuarkNet GPS primer PDF)
Net programmed offsetCombined SR + GR correction baked into clock rate~+38 µs/day netBoth

Receivers solve for position by comparing signal travel times from multiple satellites. If satellite clock rates ignored relativity, c-based ranging would systematically wrong — the QuarkNet relativity+GPS handout estimates order ~11 km/day position error without corrections (order-of-magnitude teaching figure; operational systems include additional effects).

GPS is engineering proof that relativity is not merely philosophical — it is a required correction in the Interface Control Document time scale maintained by US Space Force / NIST traceability.
